Unlike classical computers that use bits (0s and 1s), quantum computers leverage qubits that can exist in superposition states. This enables them to solve certain problems exponentially faster than traditional computers, particularly in cryptography and molecular simulation domains. However, maintaining quantum coherence remains a significant technical challenge requiring extremely low temperatures.

HARD

The Riemann Hypothesis, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, postulates that all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function ζ(s) have real part equal to 1/2. Formally, if ζ(s) = 0 and 0 < Re(s) < 1, then Re(s) = 1/2. This conjecture has profound implications for the distribution of prime numbers, as the zeta function encodes information about prime number density through its Euler product representation. Despite extensive numerical verification for billions of zeros, a general proof remains elusive after over 160 years.

In differential topology, the classification of smooth manifolds relies heavily on characteristic classes and cobordism theory. The Pontryagin classes, defined via the Chern-Weil homomorphism, provide obstructions to smoothability of topological manifolds. For oriented 4-manifolds, the intersection form's signature and Euler characteristic, related by the Hirzebruch signature theorem τ(M) = ⅓(7χ(M) + 11σ(M)), completely determine the homeomorphism type in simply-connected cases. This framework, extended through Seiberg-Witten invariants, revolutionized our understanding of 4-dimensional geometry.
